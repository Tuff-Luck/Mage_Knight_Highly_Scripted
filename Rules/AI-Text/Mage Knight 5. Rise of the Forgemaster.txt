Detailed Game Concepts
Immediate Effects
An immediate effect is an effect that resolves when it is played
(most effects are immediate). Some immediate effects allow
you to immediately play other effects. An immediate effect that
just provides Move, Influence or any types of Block or Attack, is
called a modifiable effect. In many cases, an effect is composed
of multiple effects - some that are modifiable and some that
are not. In particular - gaining Heal, Fame, Reputation, or
modifying armor or attack of a target are not modifiable effects.
Some immediate effects can be repeated a limited or unlimited
number of times as part of resolving those effects.
Delayed Effects
An effect that is not immediate is called a delayed effect. There
are two types of delayed effects: triggered effects and lasting
effects. A triggered effect is an effect that automatically
resolves when a certain event occurs or a set of conditions is
met. A lasting effect is an effect that can be resolved by the
player's choice at any time unless specified otherwise.
A delayed effect details if it is resolved a limited or unlimited
number of times. Unless specified otherwise, a delayed effect
ends at the end of the turn it was played.
Examples
- You play the Advanced Action card Path Finding to gain Move 2
and reduce the Move cost of all terrains by 1, to a minimum of 2
this turn. That card provides you with two immediate effects.
Only the Move 2 is a modifiable effect.
- You play the Spell card Burning Shield for Fire Block 4 during the
block step of combat (this is an immediate effect). You
successfully block an enemy's attack using that block and so you
will gain Fire Attack 4 during the Attack phase of combat (this is
a triggered delayed effect).

Color Scarcity
Some new effects require you to determine which colors are
scarce, or whether a specific color is scarce. A Scarce color is one of
the least common basic colors among all mana dice. This includes
dice that are taken or being used, but excludes stolen dice.
Example
- There are 3 mana dice in the game - two red dice in the Source,
and a blue mana die that you currently use on the Basic Action
card Determination. In this case, the basic colors green and
white both appear 0 times among all dice, so they are considered
the scarce colors in this situation.
- You picked the Day Tactics card Mana Steal and stole a blue
mana die. There are three white mana dice in the Source, so all
basic colors except white (blue, green, red) are considered
scarce colors now. When you take the blue die from Mana Steal
and use it, then only green and red are scarce.
- There are four dice in the Source - red white, green and blue. In
this case, all basic colors are tied for the least common basic
color, so all basic colors are considered scarce.
Tuck acard
When a player is instructed to tuck cards (usually unplayed
cards from thier hand), they reveal those cards and place them
on the bottom of their Deed deck in any order. Players cannot
tuck Wound cards, and are unable to tuck any cards while their
Deed deck is empty. When a player tucks multiple cards at the
same time, they are not required to reveal the order of these
cards to the other players.

Prepare a card
When a player is instructed to prepare cards, they place those
cards on top of their Deed deck. Players are allowed to prepare
cards even if their Deed deck is empty, and they are even
allowed to prepare Wound cards. Whenever a player prepares
cards, they are not required to reveal those cards or their order.

Elixir of Life
This module builds upon the previous modules and adds more
complex game concepts and a brand new character. This module is
intended for players who are already experienced with the

Innate Fortification
When an enemy or Unit has Innate Fortification, thematically it
means that using thier quick reflexes and armor proficiency,
they can easily block projectile attacks. When an enemy has the
Fortified ability printed on them (not gained from site
fortification), it means they have Innate Fortification.
A Unit with Innate Fortification has all its blocks count twice
against an attack with Swiftness (Utem Guardsmen and Altem
Guardians already have Innate Fortification).
An enemy with Unfortified cannot benefit from Innate
Fortification and is never considered Fortified. When an enemy's
Innate Fortification is removed, it does not affect their Site
Fortification and vice versa.
Arcane Immunity
A Unit with Arcane Immunity is immune to any additional effects
of Wounds, such as Poison, Paralyze and Vampiric. In PvP, the
Unit is immune to all special effects played by your opponents.
Arcane Vulnerability
An enemy with Arcane Vulnerablity can be affected by any
special effects regardless of other resistances. A Unit with
Arcane Vulnerability is affected by the additional effects of
Wounds, such as Poison, Paralyze, Vampiric, regardless of
whether the Unit was wounded or not after assigning damage
to them. In PvP, the Unit can be the target of any special effects
regardless of their other resistances. Opponents and bosses
(like Volkare and faction leaders) can never be affected by
Arcane Vulnerability.
A Unit or enemy that gains Arcane Immunity immediately loses
their Arcane Vulnerability, and vice versa (Arcane Immunity
does not protect against Arcane Vulnerability).

Restricted Move Points
Some effects provide a special type of Move Points that cannot
be spent
to pay terrain costs. Count these Restricted Move
Points separately than your normal Move Points, and
announce which ones you use when you need to spend Move
Points. Restricted Move Points can be used for all other uses of
Move Points: exploring, paying to move over walls, entering a

Poisonous Attacks
Some effects allow players to play Poisonous Attacks, which
deal more damage at the cost of defeating their target ata
slower pace.
One Poisonous Attack in each attack can be counted an
additional time. If the targeted enemies would be defeated,
instead their attacks are halved rounded up and they are
defeated only at the end of the combat. This means that the
player will not receive any bonuses for defeating them in that
attack or that attack phase.
Ranged and Siege Attacks phase
You cannot use a Poisonous Attack in the Ranged and Siege
Attacks phase of combat, unless it also has a type that allows
you to do so (like Ranged). When using the effect of a
Poisonous Attack in the Ranged and Siege phase of combat,
the targeted enemies have all thier attacks halved (rounded
up). Summoning Attacks ignore this effect.
Attack phase
When using the effect of a Poisonous Attack in the Attack phase,
you will receive 1 less Fame for each enemy defeated by that
attack, to a minimum of 1. If the Fame you receive is multiplied
(e.g. Druid Nights Scenario), apply this reduction afterwards.
Enemies Immune to Poisonous Attacks
Currently, only enemies with Arcane Immunity are immune to
Poisonous Attacks. If you play a Poisonous Attack during an
attack targeting at least one enemy with Arcane Immunity,
you
cannot use the effect of that Attack.
Player vs Player combat
In Player vs Player combat Poisonous Attacks work differently
than against normal enemies. When all Attacks played in a single
attack are Poisonous Attacks, all damage assigned using this
attack has the same effect as the normal Poisonous effect:
- A Unit will be assigned two Wounds instead ofone (unless it has
Arcane Immunity)
- A player will receive a Wound card to their discard pile for each
Wound card received to their hand.
Examples
- You use a Poisonous Ranged Attack 5 in the Ranged and Siege
phase combat against a Werewolf and a Minotaur. Since neither
of them has Arcane Immunity, you can count that Attack an
additional time, Ranged Attack 10 is enough to defeat them
(they have a combined armor of 10), and so they will be defeated
only at the end of the combat. The Werewolf will attack witha
Swift attack of 4 (instead of 7) and the Minotaur will attack with
a Brutal attack of 3 (instead of 5). You get the normal Fame (9).
- You fight two enemies in the same combat: Swordsmen and
